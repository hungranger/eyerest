Eye-strain basics

Why your eyes hurt after a day on the Mac, and how to fix it

If your eyes feel dry, tired, or achy by mid-afternoon, you're not imagining it. The cause is simple, and so is the fix.

The short version

Hours of close, fixed-focus screen work is the most common cause of digital eye strain (also called computer vision syndrome). The fix is free and takes twenty seconds at a time: every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds. The hard part isn't the rule, it's remembering to keep it, so the real trick is making the break something you actually want to take.

What actually causes it

  • You stop blinking. People blink far less while staring at a screen, so the eye's surface dries out.
  • Your focus never moves. Looking at one distance for hours keeps the eye's focusing muscle contracted, and it fatigues.
  • Glare and blue-heavy light add to the discomfort, though these matter less than the two above.

The result: dryness, blurriness, headaches, and that "my eyes are done" feeling at the end of the day.

The 20-20-20 rule

The most widely recommended habit for reducing eye strain is simple: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Shifting focus to something far away relaxes the focusing muscle, and the pause gives you a moment to blink and rewet your eyes. It costs almost nothing and, done consistently, makes a real difference. (More on the 20-20-20 rule and why it's so hard to keep.)

Why "just remember to do it" doesn't work

Nobody remembers to look away every 20 minutes while they're deep in work, that's the whole problem. Reminder apps exist to solve this, but most people install one and turn it off within a week, because a pop-up interrupting you with a grey overlay is unpleasant. So the habit that would help never sticks.

The trick: make the break something you want

The reason people abandon break apps isn't laziness, it's that the break is annoying. Flip that, and adherence follows. If the pause is genuinely pleasant to look at, you take it instead of dismissing it.

A few extra habits that help

  • Blink on purpose during breaks. A few slow, full blinks rewet the surface.
  • Adjust your distance. Keep the screen about an arm's length away, with the top at or just below eye level.
  • Mind the lighting. Reduce glare, and warm the screen's color in the evening (f.lux or Night Shift) as a complement, not a replacement, for actually resting your eyes.
  • The 20-20-20 rule is the anchor. The rest is supporting cast.

Your eyes do a lot of work. Twenty seconds every twenty minutes is a small thing to give them back.

Questions

What causes digital eye strain on a computer?

Mostly two things: you blink far less while staring at a screen, so your eyes dry out, and your focus stays locked at one close distance for hours, which fatigues the eye's focusing muscle. Glare and blue-heavy light add to it but matter less than those two.

How do I reduce eye strain on my Mac?

Follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for 20 seconds, and blink a few times. Keep the screen about an arm's length away with the top at or just below eye level, and reduce glare. The 20-20-20 break is the anchor; the rest is supporting cast.

Does blue light cause eye strain?

Blue-heavy light and glare can add to discomfort, but the bigger drivers are reduced blinking and constant close focus. Warming your screen at night (Night Shift or f.lux) is a helpful complement, not a replacement for actually resting your eyes.
