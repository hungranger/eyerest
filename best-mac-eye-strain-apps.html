The best Mac eye-strain and break apps in 2026

The best break app isn't the one with the most settings. It's the one you'll actually keep. Here's how the top macOS options stack up on that.

The short version

The 20-20-20 rule is simple; keeping it is the hard part. Eye Rest is the one built for adherence, it makes each break a hand-painted scene you look forward to, so you don't turn it off. LookAway is the most configurable if you like to tune every knob. Stretchly is the best free and open-source pick. f.lux shifts color temperature but doesn't make you rest, so pair it with a real break app.

What actually makes a break app work

People rarely quit a break app because it lacks features. They quit because the break is annoying or easy to ignore, so they turn it off and go back to sore eyes. A break app you'll actually keep tends to have all of these:

  1. A break worth taking, so you look forward to it instead of dismissing it.
  2. A heads-up before the break, so it does not ambush you mid-sentence.
  3. Auto-pause during meetings and video, so it does not interrupt the worst moment.
  4. A skip or snooze, so you are never trapped.
  5. Configurable timing, so it fits your work.

Use that as your checklist below.

Questions

What is the 20-20-20 rule?

Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. It gives your focusing muscles a rest and is the standard advice for digital eye strain.

Do these apps actually help eye strain?

They help if you keep them on. The rule works; adherence is the hard part, which is why whether you'll keep taking the break matters more than feature count.

Is a paid break app worth it over a free one?

Only if you'll keep using it. Free apps like Stretchly are excellent if you stick with them; Eye Rest bets that a break you look forward to is one you won't turn off.
